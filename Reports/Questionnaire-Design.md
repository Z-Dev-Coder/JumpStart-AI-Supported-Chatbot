# JumpStart AI Support — Anonymous Questionnaire (Google Forms)

Target: ~20-30 participants (online shoppers, students, classmates, friends/family, social/messaging groups). Anonymous — no name, email, or identifying fields. Estimated completion time: ~3 minutes.

**Practical note on delivery**: the prototype is local-only (no public hosting), so most participants can't interact with it live. Send the form together with a **short demo video (2-3 min screen recording)** showing 2-3 real conversations — one normal Q&A, one that triggers a clarifying question, one that triggers human handover. Participants answer based on watching that. This keeps the survey feasible for a genuine 20-30 person spread reached via messaging/social groups, and is honest about being demo-based rather than hands-on (the 3-person usability test, separately, is where real hands-on interaction happens).

Form intro text: *"This short survey asks for your reaction to a short demo video of an AI customer-support chatbot (linked above/attached). Please watch the ~2-3 min video first, then answer below. It's anonymous and takes about 3 minutes."*

---

## Section 1: Context (2 questions)

**Q1.** How often do you shop online?
*(Multiple choice: Rarely/never — A few times a year — Monthly — Weekly or more)*

**Q2.** Have you used an AI chatbot for customer support before (any company)?
*(Multiple choice: Yes, often — Yes, a few times — No, never)*

## Section 2: Answer Clarity & Trust — RQ1 (3 questions)

**Q3.** The chatbot's answers in the video seemed relevant and easy to understand.
*(5-point Likert: Strongly Disagree → Strongly Agree)*

**Q4.** The clarifying question the chatbot asked (in the demo) made sense and seemed helpful.
*(5-point Likert, + "N/A - didn't notice one")*

**Q5.** Based on the video, I would trust this chatbot's answers.
*(5-point Likert)*

## Section 3: Handover — RQ2 (2 questions)

**Q6.** It was clear from the video why the chatbot connected the customer to a human agent.
*(5-point Likert)*

**Q7.** The handover felt like a natural, well-timed part of the conversation.
*(5-point Likert)*

## Section 4: Privacy (1 question)

**Q8.** The information the chatbot asked for (e.g. order number) seemed reasonable, not excessive.
*(5-point Likert)*

## Section 5: Interface (1 question)

**Q9.** The chat interface itself looked easy to use.
*(5-point Likert)*

## Section 6: Overall — RQ3 (3 questions)

**Q10.** Overall, based on the demo, how satisfied would you be using this chatbot for support?
*(5-point Likert: Very Dissatisfied → Very Satisfied)*

**Q11.** Would you use this instead of contacting support by phone/email?
*(Multiple choice: Yes / No / Maybe)*

**Q12.** Any other comments or concerns? *(Optional, open text)*

---

## Analysis mapping (for the Findings report)

| Theme | Questions |
|---|---|
| Answer clarity/accuracy | Q3, Q4 |
| Trust | Q5 |
| Handover visibility | Q6, Q7 |
| Privacy | Q8 |
| Interface difficulty | Q9 |
| Overall satisfaction | Q10, Q11 |

Closed questions → frequencies/percentages + bar charts, per Proposal §7.10. Q1/Q2 are the natural pie-chart candidates (simple categorical splits). Q12's open text → thematic analysis grouped into the five themes above.

**Limitation to note in the report**: since responses are based on a recorded demo rather than hands-on use, this measures perceived clarity/trust/usability from observation, not actual task-completion experience — the 3-person usability test is what supplies genuine hands-on behavioural evidence, and the two should be reported separately, not merged, exactly as your Proposal §7.10 already plans.
